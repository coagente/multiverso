# 3. Adaptive Compute Allocation & Verification Scheduling

> Part of the Multiverso research corpus - https://github.com/coagente/multiverso - Cutoff: 2026-08-12

## Why this matters for Multiverso

Multiverso's core research question is a scheduling question: under a fixed total budget, dynamically decide where the next unit of compute goes — spawn another candidate world, mutate a promising one, run a cheap smoke test, run the expensive suite, launch an adversarial attack, compare two candidates against each other, stop and admit, or stop and escalate. Every other plane of the architecture (worlds, evidence, attestations) exists to give this scheduler trustworthy state to act on. This chapter maps the four literatures the scheduler must synthesize — (1) racing/successive-halving from AutoML, (2) bandits and Bayesian optimal stopping, (3) decision-theoretic metareasoning (value of computation), and (4) the 2024–2026 wave of budget-aware test-time compute — plus two cross-cutting problems Multiverso cannot dodge: statistical dependence between pieces of evidence, and multi-objective admission decisions. The bottom line, argued in detail in §3.7: as of the 2026-08-12 cutoff, **no published system jointly and adaptively allocates a fixed total budget across candidate generation AND verification for repo-level code agents with an explicit false-admission guarantee** — but the gap is narrowing fast, and several 2026 papers each hold one piece of the puzzle.

## State of the art

### 3.1 Foundations: racing, successive halving, Hyperband

The oldest mature answer to "many candidates, limited evaluation budget" comes from algorithm configuration and hyperparameter optimization. Racing algorithms evaluate a population of candidates incrementally on a stream of instances and eliminate candidates as soon as statistical evidence shows they are inferior — Hoeffding races used concentration bounds, and F-Race replaced them with the nonparametric Friedman rank test; iterated F-Race and the production-grade [irace package](https://mlopez-ibanez.github.io/irace/) add resampling of new candidates between races ([survey of automated algorithm configuration](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01651)). [Jamieson & Talwalkar](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.07943) (2015) recast this as *non-stochastic best-arm identification*: successive halving spreads budget uniformly over n arms, drops the worst half, doubles per-arm budget, and repeats — reaching comparable accuracy "an order of magnitude faster" than uniform allocation. [Hyperband](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume18/16-558/16-558.pdf) (Li et al., JMLR 2018) hedges over the unknown "how early can you tell?" parameter by running successive halving at several aggressiveness levels, and [ASHA](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05934) (Li et al., MLSys 2020) makes halving asynchronous so it scales linearly to 500 parallel workers — directly relevant to a control plane running many isolated worlds concurrently.

The mapping to Multiverso is direct: candidate worlds are arms; cheap evidence (lint, compile, smoke tests) is a low-fidelity evaluation; the full suite plus adversarial challenge is high fidelity. The crucial assumption these methods import — that low-fidelity rank predicts high-fidelity rank — is exactly the assumption LLM-generated evidence can violate (§3.5).

### 3.2 Bandits, sequential tests, and optimal stopping on program candidates

**Best-arm identification under a budget** has been applied to LLM artifacts: [TRIPLE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09723) (Shi et al., 2024) frames prompt selection as *fixed-budget* best-arm identification, explicitly budgeting LLM calls and evaluations. **Optimal stopping of sampling** is now a small subfield: [Adaptive-Consistency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11860) (Aggarwal et al., EMNLP 2023) stops self-consistency sampling when a Dirichlet/Beta posterior over answer tallies says the leader is unlikely to change; [Early-Stopping Self-Consistency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10480) (Li et al., 2024) cuts sampling 33.8–84.2% with comparable accuracy; [ReASC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02970) (2026) adds reliability-awareness. Most notably, [MARS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.12935) (2026) provides *risk-controlled* stopping for parallel test-time scaling: it monitors partial traces at checkpoints, models vote-switching probabilities adversarially, and guarantees with high probability that the early-stopped answer matches the full-budget vote, saving 25–47% of tokens. At the serving layer, [Certaindex/Dynasor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20993) (Fu et al., 2024) measures "answer stabilization" of reasoning programs and uses it to schedule token budgets across concurrent requests — up to 50% compute savings and 3.3x throughput without accuracy loss. This is scheduling for *throughput*, not for *decision quality*, but it proves certainty-driven budget reallocation works in production systems.

The most instructive precedent is not from ML at all. Stockfish's [Fishtest](https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/fishtest-wiki/Fishtest-Mathematics.html) has, since 2013, admitted or rejected every patch to the world's strongest chess engine via a Generalized Sequential Probability Ratio Test: H0 = "the patch gains 0 Elo" vs H1 = "it gains at least a small positive Elo," a running log-likelihood ratio, and play-until-conclusive semantics with controlled type-I/type-II error ([SPRT, Chess Programming Wiki](https://chessprogramming.org/Sequential_Probability_Ratio_Test)). Fishtest is a *real, shipped, evidence-native admission gate for speculative code changes* with explicit false-admission control — precisely Multiverso's ADMIT decision — but it is verification-only (it never decides to generate another candidate), single-metric (Elo), and its evidence units (games) are cheap, i.i.d., and unlimited in supply.

**Test scheduling as a bandit** also has a decade of software-engineering literature: [RETECS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04122) (Spieker et al., ISSTA 2017) learns test-case prioritization in CI via reinforcement learning from duration and failure history; [COLEMAN](https://2021.esec-fse.org/details/fse-2021-journal-first/31/A-Multi-Armed-Bandit-Approach-for-Test-Case-Prioritization-in-Continuous-Integration-) (ESEC/FSE 2021 journal-first) shows a combinatorial volatile multi-armed bandit outperforms RL while handling tests being added/removed. Facebook's [Predictive Test Selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05286) (Machalica et al., ICSE-SEIP 2019) learns which tests a change is likely to break, skipping about half of all test executions while catching over 99.9% of faulty changes — and explicitly models test flakiness. These systems allocate a *verification* budget adaptively at industrial scale, but treat the change as given: they never trade testing against generating a better candidate.

### 3.3 Decision-theoretic metareasoning: value of computation

The principled frame for Multiverso's scheduler is Russell & Wefald's rational metareasoning: choose the computation with the highest *value of computation* (VOC) — expected improvement in final decision utility minus cost — a lineage formalized in "Principles of Metareasoning" (Artificial Intelligence, 1991) and developed rigorously in [Selecting Computations: Theory and Applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2048) (Hay, Russell, Tolpin & Shimony, UAI 2012). That paper is required reading for Multiverso: it argues the metalevel problem is *not* a standard bandit (computations change beliefs about actions rather than paying rewards directly), derives finite bounds on optimal sampling policies, shows Bayesian metalevel policies beat UCB-style allocation in selection problems and Go, and exhibits a counterexample where the "optimal" policy never stops — a warning that stopping rules must be designed, not assumed.

The lineage reached LLMs in 2024–2025. [Rational Metareasoning for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05563) (De Sabbata, Sumers & Griffiths, 2024) builds a VOC-shaped reward that penalizes unnecessary reasoning and trains models with expert iteration, cutting 20–37% of generated tokens at matched performance. [Learning How Hard to Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04707) (Damani et al., ICLR 2025) trains a lightweight difficulty model — probes on the LM's hidden states — to predict the *marginal benefit* of extra decoding compute per input and route budget accordingly, across code generation, math, and dialog. [OSCA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22480) (Zhang et al., 2024) learns an optimal *mixture* of inference configurations (model, temperature, language) under a budget, achieving matched accuracy with 128x less compute on code generation. [Snell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314) (2024) established the "compute-optimal test-time scaling" result — adapting strategy per prompt difficulty improves efficiency over best-of-N by more than 4x, and a FLOPs-matched small model with test-time compute can beat a 14x larger model. In agentic settings, [Inference-Time Budget Control for LLM Search Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05701v1) (2026) assigns explicit value-of-information scores to the choices retrieve / decompose / answer under a budget, and [ZEBRA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20485) (Hamri & Talgam-Cohen, 2026) allocates a fixed *monetary* budget across pipeline phases (plan, decompose, implement, refine) by estimating per-phase utility curves and solving a continuous nonlinear knapsack via water-filling — recovering 94.4% of unconstrained quality at 50% budget on APPS coding tasks, versus 88.1% for an LLM deciding directly.

### 3.4 Budget-aware test-time compute for code and agents (2024–2026)

The empirical foundation is [Large Language Monkeys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787) (Brown et al., 2024): coverage (fraction of problems any sample solves) scales log-linearly with samples across four orders of magnitude — on SWE-bench Lite, DeepSeek-Coder-V2 goes from 15.9% (1 sample) to 56% (250 samples), beating the then-SOTA single-sample 43%. Generation is cheap and effective; *identifying* the correct sample becomes the bottleneck, which is why scheduling generation against verification is the live question.

Key mechanisms published since:

- **Early pruning of candidates.** [Speculative Rejection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20290) (2024) exploits the correlation between reward-model scores on partial and complete generations to kill weak best-of-N candidates mid-generation, 16–32x cheaper than full best-of-N. [AlphaEvolve](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/) (DeepMind, 2025; [paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2506.13131)) institutionalizes this as user-specified *evaluation cascades* — candidates face progressively harder test stages and advance only on sufficient performance — plus a breadth/depth model split (Gemini Flash for idea volume, Pro for quality).
- **Generate-new vs refine-existing as a formal bandit.** [AB-MCTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04412) (Sakana AI, 2025; [project](https://sakana.ai/ab-mcts/)) attaches a GEN node to every tree node and uses Thompson sampling over Bayesian posteriors of expected score to decide, at every step, whether to "go wider" (sample a fresh candidate) or "go deeper" (refine a promising one) given external feedback. This is the cleanest published instance of adaptive allocation *within* the generation side, later extended to multi-model ensembles on ARC-AGI-2.
- **Verification as its own scaling axis.** [Sample, Scrutinize and Scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01839) (Zhao et al., 2025) shows self-verification-based selection scales: with enough sampled verifications, Gemini v1.5 Pro's selection surpasses o1-preview, and cross-candidate *comparison* is a key verification primitive. [When To Solve, When To Verify](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01005) (Singhi et al., 2025) is the first careful *compute-optimal split* between generating solutions and generating verifications: at fixed budget, self-consistency beats generative verification until GenRM has consumed up to 8x the compute, and compute-optimal inference "favors scaling solution generation more aggressively than scaling verification" — a static answer (a curve, not a controller), on math, but the direct ancestor of Multiverso's question. [Variation in Verification](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.17995) (2025) studies when verification helps at all, and [Heteroskedastic Signals in Budgeted LLM Verification](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15841) (2026) shows the uncertainty signals used to decide *which* outputs to verify have wildly uneven quality across cost regions — cost-stratified thresholds gain up to 17 points of hit rate, and a heterogeneity-gated allocator decides when stratification is warranted. That is a caution for Multiverso: the scheduler's own routing signal needs quality monitoring.
- **Code-specific selection with execution.** [S*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14382) (Li, Cao et al., 2025) combines parallel and sequential scaling and introduces *adaptive input synthesis*: cluster candidates by execution outputs on synthesized inputs, then prompt the LLM to generate *distinguishing inputs* for pairwise cluster comparisons and pick winners by executing them — GPT-4o-mini with S* beats o1-preview by 3.7% on LiveCodeBench. This makes "compare two candidates" a first-class, cheap, execution-grounded action — exactly one of Multiverso's scheduler actions.
- **Repo-level agents.** [CodeMonkeys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14723) (Ehrlich et al., 2025) resolves 57.4% of SWE-bench Verified for ~$2,300 total by scaling serial iterations and parallel trajectories, selecting via voting with model-generated tests plus a dedicated selection trajectory; strikingly, many different serial/parallel splits give similar coverage — evidence that *static* splits are a plateau and adaptivity must come from elsewhere. [R2E-Gym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07164) (Jain et al., 2025) shows *hybrid verifiers* — execution-based (unbiased, low distinguishability) plus execution-free judges (discriminative, biased) — push open-weight agents to 51% on SWE-bench Verified, establishing that verifier composition matters as much as verifier quantity. In 2026, [SWE-Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22129) (Ding et al.) makes agentic scaling adaptive on the generation side: recycle archived trajectories and choose between exploring from scratch or branching at critical intermediate steps, cutting cost up to 17.4% while gaining up to 3.8% on SWE-bench Verified — notably *without* relying on noisy learned value estimates. [Scaling Test-Time Compute for Agentic Coding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16529) (Kim et al., 2026, Meta) argues long-horizon rollouts must be compressed into structured summaries (hypotheses, progress, failure modes) before selection can work, and gets 72.3%→76.6% on SWE-bench Verified via recursive tournament voting. [SWE-TRACE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.14820) (2026) adds rubric-based process reward models for heuristic test-time scaling of long-horizon agents.

### 3.5 Evidence is correlated: N generated tests are not N observations

Multiverso's evidence plane must confront a fact the racing/bandit literature assumes away: tests written by the same model from the same (possibly wrong) interpretation of intent are statistically dependent, and dependent with the candidate code itself. The best-developed treatments come from code selection:

- [CodeT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10397) (Chen et al., 2022) scores candidates by *dual execution agreement* — consistency with generated tests *and* agreement with other candidates — a RANSAC-like consensus that implicitly discounts idiosyncratic errors but has no explicit dependence model.
- [B4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08692) (ASE 2024) makes it explicit: when both solutions and tests are merely *plausible*, optimal selection is the posterior over the observed solution×test passing matrix; they cast it as integer programming with a bounded-error approximation and beat the strongest heuristic by up to 50% (246% over random). This is the closest thing to a published probabilistic model of "how much is this passing test actually worth?"
- [Great Models Think Alike and this Undermines AI Oversight](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04313) (Goel et al., 2025) supplies the population-level warning: their CAPA metric (chance-adjusted probabilistic agreement on *mistakes*) shows LLM judges systematically favor models similar to themselves, gains from weak-to-strong supervision come from *complementary* knowledge, and — worst for Multiverso — model errors are becoming *more* correlated as capabilities rise. Verification bought from a model correlated with the generator is partially spent money.
- [Adversarial Test-Hardening for AI-Written Code](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23002v1) (Otterson, 2026) runs a Tester→mutation-testing→Critic loop where every verdict is decided mechanically (no model judges another model's output); the loop killed 105 mutants one-shot generation missed. Its "instrument autopsy" is a methodological gift: an apparent cross-lineage effect turned out to be a *lineage-correlated instrument artifact* (an output cap silently truncating the verbose model) — exactly the class of correlated-evidence failure Multiverso's freshness/receipt machinery is designed to catch.
- Industrial test selection already models one dependence: flakiness ([Predictive Test Selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05286)), i.e., test outcomes correlated with infrastructure state rather than code correctness.

What does *not* exist: any scheduler that discounts the marginal value of the next test by its estimated correlation with evidence already purchased. B4 models correlation at final selection; nobody models it in the *allocation loop*.

### 3.6 Multi-objective admission: gates, then frontiers, then calibrated risk

Naive weighted sums of evidence scores are the default in test-time-compute papers and the wrong tool for admission decisions. Three sturdier patterns appear in the literature. (1) **Hard gates / cascades**: AlphaEvolve's [evaluation cascades](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/) impose lexicographic structure — fail the cheap stage and nothing else matters; Fishtest's [GSPRT bounds](https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/fishtest-wiki/Fishtest-Mathematics.html) are a hard statistical gate with chosen error rates. (2) **Racing with rank statistics**: [irace](https://mlopez-ibanez.github.io/irace/) eliminates on Friedman-test evidence rather than mean scores, robust to incommensurable scales. (3) **Calibrated risk control**: the [Learn then Test](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01052) framework (Angelopoulos et al., 2021) turns "pick a threshold that controls risk below α" into multiple hypothesis testing with distribution-free guarantees for any black-box predictor — the natural machinery for calibrating Multiverso's admission threshold against a historical stream of intents; 2026 work applies conformal aggregation to reasoning traces ([Pause and Reflect](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.14098)) and risk-controlled early stopping ([MARS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.12935)). No surveyed test-time-compute system combines all three (gates → Pareto/lexicographic preferences → calibrated residual risk) for code admission.

### 3.7 The key question: has anyone published a joint generation+verification scheduler for code agents?

**Strict answer: no — with three near misses.** (1) [When To Solve, When To Verify](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01005) allocates a fixed budget across generation and verification, but *statically* (a compute-optimal ratio computed offline), for math self-consistency, not code agents. (2) [ZEBRA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20485) allocates a fixed budget across agent workflow phases including implement/refine, but via pre-execution utility-curve estimates, not evidence-driven online reallocation, and without adversarial verification. (3) The closest match is [Anytime Verified Agents (AVA)](https://openreview.net/forum?id=JMDCMf7mlF) (OpenReview, 2026, status/venue unverified — the page is behind a bot check; abstract via search index): it "dynamically allocates compute across search, sampling, and verification within a user-specified budget, combining calibrated uncertainty estimation, value-of-information-guided search expansion, and selective verification cascades with early exits," evaluated on HumanEval-scale code generation. If AVA is what its abstract claims, the *concept* of VOI-driven joint allocation is no longer unclaimed — but at function level, without repo-level worlds, without adversarial challenge as an action, without correlated-evidence modeling, and without admission-rate guarantees. Everything else in the field allocates on one side only: AB-MCTS and SWE-Replay adapt generation; Fishtest, predictive test selection, MARS, and Heteroskedastic-BV adapt verification; CodeMonkeys and S* fix the pipeline. The specific Multiverso combination — an online scheduler over the action set {generate, mutate, cheap-test, full-suite, adversarial-attack, pairwise-compare, admit, escalate} for repo-level changes, with correlation-discounted evidence value and sequential false-admission control — remains unpublished.

## Implications for Multiverso design

1. **Frame the scheduler as metalevel decision-making, implement it as racing + VOC heuristics.** [Hay et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2048) show the metalevel problem is not a vanilla bandit and that naive optimal policies may never stop; Multiverso should therefore ship an ASHA-style asynchronous fidelity ladder over worlds ([ASHA](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05934)) as the robust backbone, with VOC estimates ([Rational Metareasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05563), [Learning How Hard to Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04707)) reordering the queue rather than owning termination.
2. **Adopt Fishtest's admission semantics.** The ADMIT/REJECT decision should be a sequential test with pre-registered H0/H1 and error rates ([GSPRT](https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/fishtest-wiki/Fishtest-Mathematics.html)), where "one more game" becomes "one more independent-ish evidence unit." This gives Multiverso the false-admission-rate knob no test-time-compute paper has, and the attestation should record the terminal log-likelihood ratio.
3. **Spend early budget on generation, late budget on verification.** Both [Large Language Monkeys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787) (log-linear coverage) and [When To Solve, When To Verify](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01005) (compute-optimal favors solutions over verifications) imply the same schedule shape; [CodeMonkeys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14723)' flat serial/parallel tradeoff says don't over-engineer the generation-side split — the wins are in adaptive verification and selection.
4. **Make generate-vs-mutate a Thompson-sampled GEN node** per [AB-MCTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04412), and make pairwise candidate comparison a first-class cheap action using S*-style *distinguishing inputs* ([S*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14382)) — execution-grounded discrimination is far cheaper than another full suite run.
5. **Price evidence by correlation-discounted information, not count.** Maintain a B4-style posterior over the solution×test pass matrix ([B4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08692)); require oracle diversity (different model families for generator vs test-writer vs judge) and measure it with CAPA-like mistake agreement ([Great Models Think Alike](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04313)); prefer mechanically-decided verdicts (mutation kills, differential execution) over model judgments per the [test-hardening autopsy](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23002v1). The scheduler's routing signal itself needs auditing across cost strata ([Heteroskedastic BV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15841)).
6. **Structure the decision as gates → frontier → calibrated risk.** Hard cascades first ([AlphaEvolve](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/)), rank-based elimination among survivors ([irace](https://mlopez-ibanez.github.io/irace/)), and admission thresholds calibrated on historical intent streams with distribution-free risk control ([Learn then Test](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01052)). Never a weighted sum.
7. **Compress trajectories before comparing them.** Repo-level candidates aren't directly comparable; adopt structured rollout summaries + tournament selection per [Scaling TTC for Agentic Coding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16529), and recycle partial trajectories as mutation seeds per [SWE-Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22129).

## Open questions

- **Correlation-aware VOI:** how to estimate, online and cheaply, the effective sample size of a bundle of LLM-generated tests? B4's posterior is selection-time and quadratic in candidates×tests; the scheduler needs an O(1) marginal-value estimate per prospective evidence purchase.
- **Guarantees under adaptive evidence:** GSPRT assumes evidence units are exchangeable; Multiverso's scheduler *chooses* which evidence to buy next, biasing the stream. What sequential test remains valid under adaptive, correlated sampling (anytime-valid e-values are a candidate direction)?
- **Non-stationarity across intents:** difficulty routers ([Learning How Hard to Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04707)) are trained per-distribution; repo-level intents are near-unique. Can probes transfer, or must the scheduler bootstrap from repo history?
- **Adversarial challenge as an arm:** no budget-allocation paper treats "attack the leading candidate" as a schedulable action; its VOC is negative for the candidate but positive for the decision. How to score it without double-counting?
- **Is AVA real and does it scale?** The nearest-neighbor system is under review with an inaccessible artifact; replication at repo scale (SWE-bench-class tasks, minutes-long evidence, dollars-level budgets) is open territory.
- **Whole-portfolio optimality:** OSCA/ZEBRA optimize allocation for one task or one pipeline; Multiverso's control plane schedules across *many concurrent intents* — closer to Dynasor's regime but optimizing decision quality, not throughput. Nothing published combines both.

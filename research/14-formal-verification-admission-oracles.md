# 14. Formal Methods as Admission Oracles: Verified Codegen, Diff-Time Static Analysis, and Proof-Carrying Code

> Part of the Multiverso research corpus - https://github.com/coagente/multiverso - Cutoff: 2026-08-12

## Why this matters for Multiverso

Chapter 8's oracle ladder tops out, among automated rungs, at fuzzing, LLM judges, and adversarial challenge — all of which produce *testimonial* evidence: a signed receipt asserting that an execution happened and what it printed. Chapter 7 concluded that for such evidence, cryptography delivers integrity and identity but **veracity stays out of reach**. This chapter adds the missing rungs where that conclusion breaks: a formal proof artifact is the one evidence type whose *truth*, not just its provenance, the consumer can re-check mechanically. That is precisely the trust model of **proof-carrying code** (Necula & Lee, 1996–97): an *untrusted* producer ships an artifact plus checkable evidence; the consumer verifies the evidence, never the producer ([Wikipedia: Proof-carrying code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof-carrying_code); [Necula, POPL 1997](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/263699.263712)). Multiverso's evidence-native admission is exactly this inversion, generalized from proofs to statistical and testimonial evidence — so PCC belongs in Chapter 12's ancestry alongside the already-cited Evidential Tool Bus, and the formal rungs belong in Chapter 8's ladder.

Three developments make this practical rather than aspirational. First, a 2023–2026 wave of LLM-with-verifier-in-the-loop systems (AlphaVerus, Clover, AutoVerus, SAFE, VerMCTS, DafnyBench, Verina) shows that for narrow change classes, *machine-generated code with machine-checked proofs* is a budgetable oracle today — and AlphaVerus's explicit misaligned-specification filtering is the first published anti-reward-hacking mechanism for the evidence-plane gaming gap Chapters 2 and 8 flag as unmeasured ([AlphaVerus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06176)). Second, production diff-time static analysis is a deployed, cheap, deterministic oracle rung with the best-documented fix-rate economics in the field — Meta's Infer reached ~70% fix rates at diff time versus near-zero for offline bug lists ([Scaling Static Analyses at Facebook, CACM 2019](https://research.facebook.com/publications/scaling-static-analyses-at-facebook/)). Third, the proof-artifact ecosystem (DRAT/LRAT for SAT, Alethe for SMT, Lean environments) has matured to the point where an oracle receipt can *carry its proof in CAS* and be independently re-checked by a small, sometimes formally verified checker — directly narrowing, for this oracle class only, Chapter 7's veracity gap.

## State of the art

### 14.1 Strand A — Verified code generation with LLMs, 2023–2026

**The substrate: Verus and its ecosystem.** [Verus](https://github.com/verus-lang/verus) is an SMT-based verifier for Rust: developers write specifications and Verus statically proves the executable code satisfies them. It is an unusually healthy formal-methods ecosystem: the foundational OOPSLA 2023 paper ("Verus: Verifying Rust Programs using Linear Ghost Types") was followed by "Verus: A Practical Foundation for Systems Verification" (SOSP 2024, Distinguished Artifact Award), and by a run of major verified systems built on it — **VeriSMo**, a verified security module for confidential VMs (OSDI 2024, Best Paper Award), **Anvil**, verified-liveness Kubernetes controllers (OSDI 2024, Best Paper Award), plus Atmosphere (SOSP 2025), PoWER (OSDI 2025), CortenMM (SOSP 2025, Best Paper Award), and a PLDI 2026 semantic foundation (VerusBelt) ([Verus publications and projects](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/publications-and-projects/)). The signal for Multiverso: for systems code in Rust, "the project carries machine-checked proofs" is no longer exotic — it is an OSDI/SOSP mainstream, which means changes to such projects arrive with a ready-made, maximal-trust oracle: *does the proof still go through?*

**LLMs writing the proofs.** The proof-annotation burden is the historical blocker, and it is falling fast:

- **AutoVerus** ([arXiv:2409.13082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13082), Sept 2024, rev. Aug 2025) orchestrates LLM agents mimicking expert workflows (generate → refine → debug against verifier errors) to write Verus proofs for Rust code: **>90% success on 150 non-trivial proof tasks, more than half in under 30 seconds or 3 LLM calls**. That latency/cost profile is already inside a Multiverso per-world budget.
- **SAFE** ("Automated Proof Generation for Rust Code via Self-Evolution", [arXiv:2410.15756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15756), Oct 2024, rev. Feb 2026) attacks the data-scarcity problem: synthesize tens of thousands of proofs, let the *verifier* separate valid from invalid (a perfect label source), fine-tune, and reuse failed attempts to train self-debugging. Result: **52.52% on an expert-crafted benchmark vs. 14.39% for GPT-4o** — evidence that open, specializable models can beat frontier generalists when the oracle labels the training data.
- **DafnyBench** ([arXiv:2406.08467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08467), June 2024) benchmarks the same task for Dafny: 750+ programs (~53K LOC) stripped of hints; the best model recovered enough annotations to verify **~68%**, improving with verifier-error feedback and degrading with code complexity.
- **VerMCTS** ([arXiv:2402.08147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08147), Feb 2024) put the verifier *inside* the search: a logical verifier evaluates partial programs to guide MCTS in Dafny and Coq, yielding **+30% absolute pass@5000** over repeated sampling — the direct ancestor of AlphaVerus's Treefinement and a template for verifier-guided candidate generation in Multiverso worlds.

**Closing the loop end-to-end.** Two systems matter most because they generate *code, spec, and proof together* and confront the resulting trust problem:

- **AlphaVerus** ([arXiv:2412.06176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06176), Dec 2024; Aggarwal, Parno, Welleck) bootstraps formally verified code generation in Verus without human labels or fine-tuning, via three phases: translation from a higher-resource verification language, **Treefinement** (tree search over program refinements driven by verifier feedback), and — critically — **filtering of misaligned specifications and programs to prevent reward hacking**, i.e., candidates that make the verifier say "verified" for vacuous or wrong specs. With this loop, a LLaMA-3.1-70B generates verified code on HumanEval/MBPP-derived tasks. AlphaVerus is the existence proof that (a) verifier feedback is a search signal cheap enough to run at candidate-generation time, and (b) *spec-gaming is a real observed failure mode that needs an explicit counter-mechanism* — the same lesson Chapter 8 draws for test-gaming, now at the strongest rung.
- **Clover** ("Closed-Loop Verifiable Code Generation", [arXiv:2310.17807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17807), Oct 2023, rev. Nov 2024) targets the spec-validity problem head-on with a consistency triangle: code, docstring, and formal annotation must each be derivable/consistent pairwise, checked by a mix of the Dafny verifier and LLM cross-checks. On CloverBench, Clover **accepts up to 87% of correct examples while rejecting 100% of adversarially incorrect ones (zero false positives)**, and flagged 6 incorrect programs inside the existing human-written MBPP-DFY-50 dataset. Acceptance-vs-rejection asymmetry of this shape is exactly what an admission gate wants: conservative, with a measured false-accept rate of zero on its benchmark.
- **Verina** ([arXiv:2505.23135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23135), May 2025, rev. Mar 2026) is the sobering calibration: 189 curated Lean tasks scoring code, spec, and proof generation jointly. Best model (o3): **72.6% code correctness, 52.3% spec soundness+completeness, but only 4.9% proof success** (single trial). General-purpose "generate code with proof" is not a routine rung; domain-specialized pipelines (AutoVerus/SAFE-class) are years ahead of general ones.

**Spec generation as its own evidence type.** [nl2postcond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01831) (FSE 2024) showed LLM-generated formal postconditions from natural language are useful *discriminators*: generated postconditions caught 64 real historical bugs in Defects4J. Specs synthesized from an intent — even without full verification — are evidence with measurable discriminative power, sitting naturally beside Chapter 8's PBT properties. Adjacent but distinct: **VeriCoder** ([arXiv:2504.15659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15659), Apr 2025) applies test-validated fine-tuning to *RTL/Verilog* generation (125,777-example dataset, +71.7% relative on VerilogEval) — "verified" there means test-passing, not proof-carrying, a reminder to read claims of "verified codegen" carefully.

**Which change classes have a budgetable proof rung today?** Composing the above: (i) **already-verified codebases** (Verus/Dafny/Lean/F* projects, the s2n proof suite) — every change has a maximal-trust oracle at near-zero marginal spec cost, and AutoVerus/SAFE-class agents can attempt automatic proof *repair*; (ii) **invariant-rich, self-contained modules** — parsers, protocol state machines, codecs, data structures, access-control logic, memory-safety-critical `unsafe` blocks — where AlphaVerus/Clover-style spec synthesis plus verification is feasible at function-to-module granularity; (iii) **everything else** — no spec, no proof rung; the ladder stops at testing. The scheduler needs an *applicability predicate*, not a universal rung.

### 14.2 Strand B — Production static analysis as admission evidence

**The deployment result that transfers directly.** Meta's CACM 2019 paper reports the defining natural experiment: the same Infer analyses that achieved a **~70% fix rate when reported at diff time** (in code review, on the changed code) had achieved **near-zero action rates when delivered as offline bug lists**; Infer has had >100K issues fixed before reaching production, and Zoncolan (taint analysis over ~100M lines of Hack) "outperform[ed] any other detection method used at Facebook" for security bugs ([Scaling Static Analyses at Facebook](https://research.facebook.com/publications/scaling-static-analyses-at-facebook/)). Google's Tricorder experience matches: analyzers surfaced at review time lead to thousands of issues per day fixed by engineers *by choice* before check-in ([Lessons from Building Static Analysis Tools at Google, CACM 2018](https://research.google/pubs/lessons-from-building-static-analysis-tools-at-google/)). The transferable law: **oracle value is a steep function of when in the change lifecycle it fires.** Multiverso's architecture applies every oracle at candidate time by construction — it inherits the 0→70% effect for free, and this literature is the citation for why that architectural choice matters.

**LLM-hybrid static analysis (2023–2026).** The FP-economics problem is being attacked from both ends. On the *fix* side, GitHub's **Copilot Autofix** (public beta March 2024) attaches CodeQL to Copilot to propose remediations covering "more than 90% of alert types in JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, and Python," with suggestions that "remediate more than two-thirds of found vulnerabilities with little or no editing" ([GitHub blog, Mar 2024](https://github.blog/news-insights/product-news/found-means-fixed-introducing-code-scanning-autofix-powered-by-github-copilot-and-codeql/)); at GA (Aug 14, 2024), beta telemetry showed median remediation of **28 minutes vs. 90 manual (3×), XSS 22 vs. ~180 (7×), SQL injection 18 vs. 222 minutes (12×)** ([GitHub blog, Aug 2024](https://github.blog/news-insights/product-news/secure-code-more-than-three-times-faster-with-copilot-autofix/)). Microsoft's **InferFix** ([arXiv:2303.07263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07263)) chains Infer detection to a retrieval-augmented fine-tuned model inside Microsoft's CI, at 65.6% (C#) and 76.8% (Java) top-1 fix accuracy. On the *detection* side, **IRIS** ([arXiv:2405.17238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17238), rev. 2025) uses an LLM to infer the taint specifications CodeQL lacks for third-party APIs: on CWE-Bench-Java (120 validated vulnerabilities), IRIS+GPT-4 detects **55 vs. CodeQL's 27**, improves false-discovery rate by 5 points, and found 4 previously unknown vulnerabilities. The pattern for Multiverso: static analyzers are the *deterministic skeleton*; LLMs fill their specification gaps and consume their outputs — never the reverse.

**Gate mechanics are commodity.** Semgrep's policy model is representative: rules run in **Monitor / Comment / Block** modes; Block returns exit code 1 and, combined with branch protection, blocks the merge, in both full and diff-aware scans ([Semgrep policies docs](https://docs.semgrep.dev/semgrep-code/policies)). GitHub code scanning similarly runs on push/PR events and surfaces alerts on the PR ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/code-scanning/introduction-to-code-scanning/about-code-scanning)). Diff-scoped, block-capable analysis with per-rule policy is a solved integration problem; Multiverso only has to *receipt* it.

**Amazon's automated-reasoning fleet: formal methods as a production service.** AWS runs the largest deployed portfolio of admission-shaped formal tools: **Zelkova** encodes IAM policy semantics into SMT and is "invoked many millions of times daily" answering a PSPACE-complete question about access policies ([FMCAD 2018](https://www.amazon.science/publications/semantic-based-automated-reasoning-for-aws-access-policies-using-smt)); **s2n's proofs run in CI** — the CAV 2018 paper reports HMAC/DRBG/TLS-handshake correctness proofs (SAW, Cryptol, Coq) wired into Travis CI, with **956 re-verification runs over a year requiring only 3 manual proof updates** ([Continuous Formal Verification of Amazon s2n, CAV 2018](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-96142-2_26)) — the single best published datum on the *maintenance* cost of proof evidence under code change; **Kani** is AWS's bit-precise bounded model checker for Rust built on CBMC, checking undefined behavior, panics, overflow, and user contracts via `cargo kani`, with a first-party GitHub Action ([Kani](https://github.com/model-checking/kani)); the **verify-rust-std challenge** (announced Nov 20, 2024, hosted with the Rust Foundation) crowdsources contracts and proofs for the Rust standard library — ~35,000 functions, ~7,500 of them unsafe, motivated by 57 soundness issues and 20 CVEs in three years ([AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/opensource/verify-the-safety-of-the-rust-standard-library/)); **Cedar**, the authorization language, was built by "verification-guided development" — a Lean model with machine-checked design properties, differentially tested against the production Rust engine ([arXiv:2403.04651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04651)); and Bedrock Guardrails' **Automated Reasoning checks** (preview Dec 3, 2024) compile documents into formal-logic policies and validate LLM outputs against them ([AWS News blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/prevent-factual-errors-from-llm-hallucinations-with-mathematically-sound-automated-reasoning-checks-preview/)). The meta-lesson: formal tools scale operationally when they are (a) domain-narrow, (b) fully automatic, and (c) run at a decision point — the same three predicates Multiverso's scheduler should encode.

**Soundness tier and invalidation basis.** None of these tools is "sound" simpliciter: Infer and CodeQL are unsound-by-design bug hunters tuned for precision; Semgrep diff-aware mode drops cross-file analysis ([Semgrep docs](https://docs.semgrep.dev/semgrep-code/policies)); Kani is exhaustive only within loop/harness bounds ([Kani](https://github.com/model-checking/kani)); Zelkova is sound with respect to its policy-language model ([FMCAD 2018](https://www.amazon.science/publications/semantic-based-automated-reasoning-for-aws-access-policies-using-smt)). For the evidence plane this is a feature, not an embarrassment, *provided the receipt records the tier*: "no findings, per CodeQL ruleset digest R, diff-scope S" is a precise, replayable claim. Static-analysis facts also have the sharpest **invalidation bases** in the whole ladder — a finding depends on an identifiable code region, rule version, and analysis configuration — which plugs directly into Chapter 11's `staleness(Evidence, Change)` derivation: rule-set digest changed → stale; analyzed region untouched by diff → fresh (with a cross-file caveat matching each analyzer's scope).

### 14.3 Strand C — Proof-carrying code and its descendants

**The archetype.** Necula and Lee's proof-carrying code (OSDI 1996; POPL 1997) let an OS kernel accept *machine code from an untrusted producer*: the kernel publishes a safety policy; the producer ships code plus a formal proof; the kernel runs a fast proof checker. Tampering either breaks the proof or leaves the code still-safe — trust derives entirely from the checkable artifact, not from the producer's identity or process ([Wikipedia: PCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof-carrying_code); [Necula, POPL 1997](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/263699.263712)). **Foundational PCC** (Appel, LICS 2001) then asked *quis custodiet*: the original PCC trusted a type system and a verification-condition generator; FPCC shrinks the trusted base to "the foundational verification system together with the definition of the machine instruction semantics and the safety policy," checking proofs in bare higher-order logic ([Appel, FPCC](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~appel/papers/fpcc.pdf)). The two-step is instructive for Multiverso: first invert trust (evidence, not producer), then *minimize the checker* — Chapter 7's residual trust items are Multiverso's un-shrunk TCB.
 
**Shipped descendants.** The in-kernel **eBPF verifier** is PCC's pragmatic cousin at planetary scale: every BPF program is statically verified at load time (CFG checks, register-type and bounds tracking, path simulation) and rejected outright on failure — admission-time verification of untrusted programs at a hard trust boundary, with the consumer running the analysis itself rather than checking a shipped proof ([kernel docs](https://docs.kernel.org/bpf/verifier.html)). **CompCert** established that the *toolchain itself* can carry proofs — a C compiler with a Coq-verified semantic-preservation theorem ([Leroy, CACM 2009](https://xavierleroy.org/publi/compcert-CACM.pdf)) — which matters to Multiverso because oracle receipts implicitly trust compilers. And the **Evidential Tool Bus** (Rushby 2005), already cited in Chapter 12, is PCC's softer sibling: heterogeneous tools contribute evidence with provenance into an assurance case ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/11576280_3)); PCC is the limit case where one evidence item is self-certifying.

**The proof-artifact ecosystem.** The critical enabler for making Multiverso receipts *re-checkable* is that proof formats and small checkers now exist across the solver stack: SAT solvers emit **DRAT** clausal proofs, "used to validate the results of the SAT competitions" via drat-trim ([drat-trim](https://github.com/marijnheule/drat-trim)); **cake_lpr** is an LRAT/LPR checker *formally verified* down through the CakeML compiler ([cake_lpr](https://github.com/tanyongkiam/cake_lpr)); SMT solvers (cvc5, veriT) emit **Alethe** proofs checked and elaborated by **Carcara** (TACAS 2023) ([Carcara](https://github.com/ufmg-smite/carcara)); and Lean 4 environments can be replayed through the kernel by an external checker — lean4checker, integrated into Lean 4.28 as `leanchecker` — expressly to defeat "environment hacking" that bypasses the kernel ([lean4checker](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4checker)). At the exotic end, zkVMs make *arbitrary execution* proof-carrying: a RISC Zero **receipt** (journal + seal) lets a third party verify that a specific program (image ID) honestly produced specific outputs, without re-executing ([RISC Zero docs](https://dev.risczero.com/api/zkvm/receipts)) — testimonial evidence upgraded to machine-checkable veracity, today at costs far beyond CI economics (author's assessment).

## Implications for Multiverso design

**1. Extend the Chapter 8 ladder with four formal rungs, each with an applicability predicate.** (Costs are author's engineering estimates, like Chapter 8's, except where cited.)

| Rung | Oracle | Marginal cost / candidate (est.) | Latency | Trust for what it checks | Applicability predicate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1′ (replaces Ch8 rung 1) | Diff-time static analysis: pattern (Semgrep-block), semantic query (CodeQL), compositional interprocedural (Infer), LLM-augmented (IRIS/Autofix) | ~$0.01–0.1 | seconds–minutes | Deterministic; unsound-by-design, precision known per rule; ~70% fix-rate economics at diff time ([CACM 2019](https://research.facebook.com/publications/scaling-static-analyses-at-facebook/)) | any supported language; always-on |
| 7′ (new, beside fuzzing) | Bounded model checking on the diff (Kani/CBMC harness for changed functions) | ~$0.1–1 CPU | minutes–hours | Exhaustive within bounds; sound for checked property classes (UB, panics, contracts) under harness assumptions ([Kani](https://github.com/model-checking/kani)) | language ∈ {Rust, C/C++}; harness exists or is LLM-generable for the diff |
| 9′ (new) | Proof re-establishment in already-specified code, with LLM proof repair (AutoVerus/SAFE-class) on breakage | verifier run ≈ $0.01–0.1; repair ≈ $0.1–5 | seconds–minutes (s2n: 3 manual interventions in 956 CI runs/yr ([CAV 2018](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-96142-2_26))) | Highest automated trust: full functional property, machine-checked | repo already carries specs (Verus/Dafny/Lean/F*/SAW) covering the diff |
| 11 (new top automated rung) | Spec synthesis + verification for unspecified diffs (AlphaVerus/Clover-class), **paired with mandatory spec-validity evidence** | ~$1–50 per module | tens of minutes | Proof is absolute for the spec; net trust bounded by spec validity — hence Clover's 87%/100% accept/reject asymmetry ([Clover](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17807)) | invariant-rich, self-contained module (parser, protocol FSM, codec, data structure, authz logic); property expressible in the verifier's logic |

Two ladder-level notes. First, rung 9′/11 trust is *conditional*, like rung 0's: absolute for what it checks, silent about everything else (side effects outside the spec, performance, the build). Second, Verina's 4.9% proof-success calibration ([Verina](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23135)) means rung 11 is a *narrow-applicability, high-cost* rung the scheduler buys only when the applicability predicate fires — exactly the kind of decision an evidence-aware scheduler exists to make, and a new arm class for the Chapter 3 budget question.

**2. Carry the proof artifact in CAS; grade receipts by re-checkability.** Define a veracity-tier field on every oracle receipt:

- **V0 — testimonial**: receipt asserts an execution (all Chapter 8 rungs today). Veracity unverifiable; Chapter 7's conclusion applies in full.
- **V1 — replayable**: receipt + pinned environment digests allow deterministic re-execution; veracity checkable at full re-run cost. (Verus/Dafny/Kani gates today: the pipeline trusts Z3/CBMC's "UNSAT/verified" verdict and exports no certificate.)
- **V2 — certificate-carrying**: the solver's proof object ships in the receipt — DRAT for SAT ([drat-trim](https://github.com/marijnheule/drat-trim)), Alethe for SMT checked by Carcara ([Carcara](https://github.com/ufmg-smite/carcara)) — checkable by an independent, cheaper-than-solving checker.
- **V3 — foundational**: proof term checked by a minimal or formally verified kernel — Lean environments replayed by `leanchecker` ([lean4checker](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4checker)), LRAT checked by cake_lpr ([cake_lpr](https://github.com/tanyongkiam/cake_lpr)); FPCC is the design ideal ([Appel](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~appel/papers/fpcc.pdf)); zkVM receipts extend V3 to arbitrary execution at (today) prohibitive cost ([RISC Zero](https://dev.risczero.com/api/zkvm/receipts)).

Concretely: add `proofArtifact: { format: drat|lrat|alethe|lean-env|none, digest, checker, checkerDigest, veracityTier }` to the Chapter 7 world-evidence predicate. V2/V3 receipts are the **only oracle receipts whose veracity — not merely integrity — is machine-checkable at decision time and at any later audit**, which partially retracts Chapter 7's "no veracity by cryptography alone" for this oracle class and gives the trust plane its first rung where the runner need not be trusted at all: a compromised runner cannot forge a checkable proof, only refuse to produce one. Residual TCB, to be stated in the attestation: the checker (minimized at V3), the formal semantics of the language/logic, and — irreducibly — the binding between spec and intent. Practical caveats: DRAT/Alethe artifacts can be large (store in CAS by digest with retention policy, elaborate DRAT→LRAT to shrink and strengthen); Verus/Dafny would need solver-certificate export wired in (cvc5's Alethe path exists; this is engineering, not research).

**3. Static analysis slots perfectly into Chapter 11's staleness derivation.** Each finding (or clean verdict) has an explicit invalidation basis: (rule-set digest, analyzer version, analyzed region, configuration). Diff-scoped re-analysis is cheap enough to re-run per world; whole-repo interprocedural passes can be cached per trunk snapshot and invalidated by rdeps of the diff — the same machinery as Chapter 11's coverage maps, with a *tighter* basis. Record the analysis scope (diff-aware vs full, cross-file on/off per [Semgrep's own caveat](https://docs.semgrep.dev/semgrep-code/policies)) in the receipt, since it changes the claim's strength.

**4. Import the anti-reward-hacking mechanisms as evidence-plane requirements.** Chapters 2 and 8 flag evidence gaming as unmeasured. Strand A supplies the two published counter-mechanisms: AlphaVerus's **misaligned-spec filtering** (reject candidates whose specs are vacuous/exploitable before they earn "verified" status) and Clover's **consistency triangle** (spec must agree with docstring and code independently). Multiverso should treat any synthesized spec as *itself a candidate artifact* requiring its own evidence: consistency checks, nl2postcond-style discriminative-power measurement against known-buggy variants ([nl2postcond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01831)), and exposure to the Chapter 8 adversarial-challenge rung (an attacker that satisfies the spec while violating the intent falsifies the spec, not the code).

**5. Chapter 12 positioning update: add PCC as ancestor.** The comparison table in Chapter 12 should gain a row — PCC (1996): C4 **✔ in its strongest known form** (evidence bound to the exact artifact *and* veracity machine-checkable), C1/C2/C3/C5 absent — and the "assurance cases at machine speed" framing should be extended: *Multiverso attestations are proof-carrying code generalized to statistical and testimonial evidence, with the assurance tier made explicit per evidence item* (V0–V3 above). Where PCC's consumer checks one self-certifying proof, Multiverso's admission gate fuses a portfolio of evidence of heterogeneous checkability — ETB's bus ([Rushby](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/11576280_3)) with PCC's trust inversion at the top tier. This converts "you reinvented PCC" into lineage, and strengthens the veracity story: the ladder now has a top rung where the gap is closed, and a documented gradient below it.

**6. Adopt the fleet lessons for scheduling.** Amazon's portfolio shows formal oracles win when domain-narrow, automatic, and decision-point-resident ([Zelkova](https://www.amazon.science/publications/semantic-based-automated-reasoning-for-aws-access-policies-using-smt); [s2n](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-96142-2_26)); Meta/Google show delivery timing dominates analyzer strength ([CACM 2019](https://research.facebook.com/publications/scaling-static-analyses-at-facebook/); [CACM 2018](https://research.google/pubs/lessons-from-building-static-analysis-tools-at-google/)). For the scheduler: rung 1′ is always-on (cost floor, deterministic, independent of the generator's model family — it buys the *independence* Chapter 8 says to price); rungs 7′/9′/11 are gated by applicability predicates evaluated per intent, and their receipts should be weighted not just by trust but by veracity tier, since a V2+ receipt survives runner compromise that would invalidate every V0 receipt in the same world.

## Open questions

- **Proof-carrying receipts end-to-end:** can a Verus/Dafny gate be made V2 in practice — exporting solver certificates (cvc5/Alethe) at acceptable overhead and checking them with Carcara inside the admission gate — and what is the measured cost delta vs. V1 replay? No published system does this today.
- **Spec-validity as a measurable quantity:** Clover's 100% rejection is on hand-built adversarial examples; AlphaVerus's filter is evaluated on competition-style tasks. What is the spec-gaming rate of frontier agents on *repository-scale* changes, and can discriminative-power testing (nl2postcond-style) be made a routine receipt field?
- **Proof repair under COMPOSE:** merging two worlds invalidates both proofs; is LLM proof repair (AutoVerus/SAFE-class) reliable enough post-merge, or does rung 9′ force SERIALIZE for verified codebases?
- **Cost curves for rung 11:** AutoVerus reports ≤3 LLM calls typical on benchmark tasks; nothing is published on spec-synthesis+verification cost for real diffs in real repos. Multiverso instrumenting this would be a novel contribution, mirroring Chapter 8's cost-ground-truth question.
- **The checker in the TCB:** V3 requires distributing verified checkers (cake_lpr, leanchecker) as pinned, attested binaries — who signs the checker, and does it belong in the same TUF-style root as Chapter 7's policy deny-lists?
- **zkVM economics:** at what re-execution cost multiple does a zk receipt for a *test run* (upgrading rung 2 evidence from V0 to V3) become cheaper than the trust apparatus it replaces? Current overheads make this a monitoring item, not a design input.

# 2. Best-of-N Generation & Candidate Selection

> Part of the Multiverso research corpus - https://github.com/coagente/multiverso - Cutoff: 2026-08-12

## Why this matters for Multiverso

Multiverso's SELECT regime is exactly the problem this literature studies: spawn N candidate Worlds from one Intent, gather Evidence about each, and admit one. Two empirical facts anchor the whole design space. First, **coverage** (pass@k — the probability that *some* candidate is correct) scales remarkably predictably with N, following log-linear "inference scaling laws" over four orders of magnitude ([Large Language Monkeys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)). Second, **selection** (selection@k — the probability the system *picks* a correct candidate) lags coverage badly, and the gap is the single largest source of wasted compute in best-of-N systems: CodeMonkeys generated a correct edit for 69.8% of SWE-bench Verified issues but could only select one 57.4% of the time ([CodeMonkeys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14723)). Every point of that gap is compute Multiverso paid for and then threw away at the Decision step. The state of the art in closing it — execution-grounded voting, behavioral clustering, dedicated selector trajectories, learned scoring models — is precisely the menu of Evidence mechanisms Multiverso's scheduler must price under a fixed budget. And the leading 2025–2026 SWE-bench entries are, without exception, best-of-N-plus-selection systems, which means SELECT is no longer a research curiosity: it is how frontier results are actually produced, just without Multiverso's auditability, budget-awareness, or admission semantics.

## State of the art

### Repeated sampling: coverage is cheap and predictable

[Large Language Monkeys: Scaling Inference Compute with Repeated Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787) (Brown, Juravsky, Ehrlich et al., Stanford, July 2024) established the baseline economics of best-of-N. Coverage grows log-linearly with sample count and can be modeled with an exponentiated power law. On SWE-bench Lite, DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct goes from 15.9% with one sample to 56% with 250 samples — beating the then-SOTA single-attempt score of 43% with a much cheaper model. On CodeContests, Gemma-2B coverage rises over 300x, from 0.02% at one sample to 7.1% at 10,000 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)).

The same paper quantified the selection problem in verifier-free domains: on MATH, Llama-3-8B-Instruct coverage climbs from 82.9% (100 samples) to 98.44% (10,000 samples), yet majority voting plateaus around 40.5%, and reward-model weighted voting moves only from 40.50% to 41.41% over that entire range ([Large Language Monkeys, §verification](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21787v3)). Where automatic verifiers exist (code with tests, formal proofs), coverage gains convert directly to solved problems; where they don't, extra samples are nearly worthless. This is the strongest single argument for Multiverso's evidence plane: **sampling without verification capacity is a budget sink.**

### CodeMonkeys: the anatomy of a SELECT pipeline

[CodeMonkeys: Scaling Test-Time Compute for Software Engineering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14723) (Ehrlich, Brown, Juravsky, Clark, Ré, Mirhoseini, Stanford Scaling Intelligence Lab, January 2025) is the most completely instrumented SELECT system published, and its numbers are worth memorizing:

- **Generation.** For each SWE-bench Verified issue, 10 parallel (testing, editing) state-machine pairs run with claude-3-5-sonnet-20241022; each jointly writes a reproduction-test script and a candidate edit, iterating serially against execution feedback. Context localization is done cheaply by scanning every file with Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct ([paper, HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14723v1)).
- **Coverage vs selection.** Coverage (oracle selection over the 10 candidates): **69.8%**. Random selection: **45.8%**. Voting with 10 model-generated tests over 10 edits: ~53%. The full method — test-based voting to filter to top-3, then a dedicated multi-turn *selection state machine* that can write **new** differentiating tests and run them — reaches **57.4%**, "closing approximately half of the gap between random and oracle selection" ([CodeMonkeys](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14723v1)).
- **Budget split.** Total cost ≈ **$2,291.90**; generation (editing + testing state machines) consumed 78.8% ($1,805.01), selection just 5.8% ($131.95) ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14723v1)). Selection is dramatically under-provisioned relative to the value it destroys — a static allocation Multiverso's scheduler should treat as the null hypothesis to beat.
- **Barrel of Monkeys (ensemble-of-systems).** Pooling CodeMonkeys candidates with the top-4 leaderboard submissions at the time (Blackbox AI Agent, CodeStory, Learn-by-interact, devlo) raises coverage to **80.8%**; running the same selection machinery over that pool scores **66.2%**, beating the best individual member (62.8%) ([CodeMonkeys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14723)). This is the canonical demonstration that a good selector turns *other people's agents* into an ensemble — heterogeneous candidate pools plus one selector beat any single pipeline.

### Selection mechanisms: from voting to learned verifiers

**Execution-based voting and clustering.** [CodeT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10397) (Microsoft, 2022) generates both solutions and tests, then ranks by *dual execution agreement* (a solution scores by the tests it passes weighted by how many other solutions agree with its outputs), lifting code-davinci-002 HumanEval pass@1 to 65.8% — +18.8 absolute. [AlphaCode](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/359254438_Competition-Level_Code_Generation_with_AlphaCode) (DeepMind, 2022) scaled this to ~1M samples per problem: filter on example tests (removing ~99%), then cluster survivors by behavior on model-generated inputs and submit one program from each of the 10 largest clusters. [AlphaCode 2](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/AlphaCode2/AlphaCode2_Tech_Report.pdf) (December 2023) added a fine-tuned Gemini Pro **scoring model** that assigns each sample an estimated correctness score in [0,1] and picks the best candidate per cluster.

**Hybrid selection.** [S*: Test Time Scaling for Code Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14382) (Li, Cao et al., February 2025; EMNLP 2025 Findings) combines parallel sampling with sequential iterative debugging against public tests, then selects via *adaptive input synthesis*: synthesize test inputs, execute all candidates, cluster by output signature, then run **pairwise comparisons across clusters** in which the LLM is asked to generate a *distinguishing input*, both candidates are executed on it, and the winner is chosen from execution results rather than judge opinion ([NovaSky blog](https://novasky-ai.github.io/posts/S*/)). S* beats majority voting by 8.4–18.2% across Qwen models; it lets GPT-4o-mini outperform o1-preview by 3.7% on LiveCodeBench and pushes DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B to 85.7% (vs o1's 88.5%) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14382)). The design lesson: LLM judgment is useful for *proposing experiments*, not for *rendering verdicts*.

**LLM-as-judge and its pathologies.** Pure judge-based selection is measurably unreliable. [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) (Zheng et al., 2023) documented **position bias**, **verbosity bias**, **self-enhancement bias**, and weak math/reasoning grading — while noting GPT-4 judges reach >80% agreement with humans on chat preference (matching human-human agreement), which is exactly the regime code selection is *not* in. [LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076) (Panickssery, Bowman, Feng, April 2024) showed GPT-4 and Llama 2 have non-trivial self-recognition accuracy and a **linear correlation between self-recognition capability and self-preference strength** — a direct threat to any design where the generator model judges its own candidates. [Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819) (2024) traces the bias to perplexity: judges favor low-perplexity (familiar) text regardless of authorship. For Multiverso this means judge-only Evidence should carry low weight in admission policies, and generator/judge identity separation belongs in the trust plane.

**Learned verifiers.** [LEVER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08468) (ICML 2023) trains a verifier over (problem, program, execution result) triples and improves code-davinci-002 by 4.6–10.9% across four language-to-code benchmarks — evidence that execution results are the most informative verifier feature. [Dynamic Scaling of Unit Tests for Code Reward Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01054) (January 2025) shows the *number* of generated unit tests is itself a scaling axis for reward quality: their CodeRM-8B test generator plus difficulty-aware dynamic test allocation yields +18.43% for Llama3-8B on HumanEval Plus (+3.42% for GPT-4o-mini), with the biggest gains on the hardest problems. This is a scheduler result in disguise: **verification compute should be allocated per-instance by difficulty**, which is Multiverso's core research bet applied to one evidence type. At the frontier, Anthropic's "parallel test-time compute" numbers use the same recipe productized: sample multiple attempts, "discard patches that break the visible regression tests in the repository… then use an internal scoring model to select the best candidate" ([Claude Sonnet 4.5 announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-4-5), 2025).

### Diversity: does a different family beat more samples?

The evidence is genuinely split, and the split is informative:

- **Plan-level diversity beats temperature.** [PlanSearch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03733) (Scale AI, September 2024) argues repeated sampling wastes budget on near-duplicate wrong answers; searching over *natural-language observations and plans* before coding yields pass@200 of 77.0% on LiveCodeBench with Claude 3.5 Sonnet vs 60.6% for standard repeated sampling (pass@1 41.4%) — and search gains across all models/benchmarks are "accurately predicted as a direct function of the diversity over generated ideas." Diversity should be *engineered upstream* (idea/plan space), not left to the sampler.
- **Cross-family ensembles win on SWE tasks.** [Diversity Empowers Intelligence (DEI)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07060) (August 2024) runs an LLM code-review committee over candidates produced by *different agent frameworks*: a group of open-source agents whose best member resolves 27.3% of SWE-bench Lite reaches **34.3%** under DEI re-ranking (+25% relative); their best committee hit 55%. [Multi-LLM AB-MCTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04412) (Sakana AI, July 2025) makes model choice a *search decision*: on ARC-AGI-2 at 250 LLM calls, repeated sampling with o4-mini solves 23%, single-model AB-MCTS 27.5%, and the o4-mini + Gemini-2.5-Pro + DeepSeek-R1-0528 combination over 30%, with problems no single model could solve falling to the ensemble ([Sakana blog](https://sakana.ai/ab-mcts/)).
- **But mixing can hurt.** [Rethinking Mixture-of-Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00674) (Princeton, February 2025) found that **Self-MoA** — aggregating repeated samples from the *single best* model — outperforms mixed-model MoA by 6.6% on AlpacaEval 2.0 and 3.8% on average across MMLU/CRUX/MATH, because MoA "is rather sensitive to quality" and mixing drags average candidate quality down.

Synthesis of the split: heterogeneity pays when (a) families have genuinely complementary failure modes (SWE agents, frontier reasoning models on ARC) *and* (b) the selector is execution-grounded enough to reject the weaker family's junk. When one family dominates on quality and the aggregator is judge-like rather than verifier-like, diversity is negative-value. For Multiverso: model/family heterogeneity is a scheduler *action* whose payoff depends on measured complementarity, not a fixed config.

### Candidate synthesis: merging candidates into a new one (COMPOSE precursors)

Work on *fusing* candidates is thin compared to selection. [LLM-Blender](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02561) (ACL 2023) is the classic template: PairRanker (a DeBERTa cross-encoder doing pairwise comparison) ranks candidates, then **GenFuser** (Flan-T5-XL) generates a new output fusing the top-K — but this is instruction-following text, not repo-level patches. [AlphaEvolve](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/) (DeepMind, May 2025) is the strongest code-domain example: an evolutionary loop where a prompt sampler assembles prompts containing *multiple previous programs* from a program database, Gemini Flash/Pro propose variants, automated evaluators score them, and the database's evolutionary algorithm decides which programs seed future generations — effectively recombination under execution-grounded fitness. Results: a 48-scalar-multiplication algorithm for 4×4 complex matrix multiplication (beating Strassen's 1969 construction), recovery of 0.7% of Google's worldwide compute via scheduling heuristics, a 23% matmul-kernel speedup (1% off Gemini training time), and up to 32.5% FlashAttention kernel speedup ([DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/)). Notably absent from the literature: a system that merges *parts of several SWE-bench-style patches* (e.g., hunk-level recombination guided by per-hunk evidence) into a new candidate. TRAE's AST-equivalence clustering (below) and CodeMonkeys' differentiating tests provide the ingredients — behavioral equivalence classes over patch fragments — but nobody has shipped COMPOSE for repository patches. (unverified as absence: no such system found by this survey.)

### What top SWE-bench Verified entries actually do (2025–2026)

- **TRAE (ByteDance), 70.6%, May 2025.** Candidates from three model families (Claude 3.7 Sonnet: 60.6–62.6% solo; o4-mini: 54.4–55.8%; Gemini 2.5 Pro: 52.4–55%); a **Tester agent** filters patches failing regression tests; a **Selector agent** does "syntax-based voting" — clustering patches by AST equivalence — followed by multi-selection voting across several selector instances. This multi-agent voting pipeline *replaced* an earlier plain o1 LLM-as-selector ([ByteDance SE Lab blog](https://se-research.bytedance.com/blogs/trae-on-swe-bench-verified-71)). Ensemble gain over best solo model: ~8 points.
- **Leaderboard profile, July 2025.** [Dissecting the SWE-Bench Leaderboards](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17208v2) records ByteDance at **75.2%** (candidates from Claude 3.7 Sonnet + Claude 4 Sonnet + Claude 4 Opus + Gemini 2.5 Pro), Refact.ai at 74.4%, Anthropic at 73.2%; most submissions still use a single LLM, but the SOTA entries are increasingly multi-model generate-and-select pipelines.
- **Anthropic, Claude Sonnet 4.5 (2025): 77.2% single-run methodology → 82.0% with parallel test-time compute** — multiple sampled attempts, rejection of patches breaking visible regression tests (explicitly Agentless-style, no hidden-test leakage), then an internal scoring model picks the winner ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-4-5)). Best-of-N-plus-learned-verifier is now how frontier labs report their headline coding numbers.
- For calibration, CodeMonkeys' January 2025 paper noted OpenAI's o3 reported 71.7% on SWE-bench Verified as a single-agent result ([CodeMonkeys](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14723v1)) — i.e., ensembles of weaker models have repeatedly matched or beaten the next frontier model months early, at disclosed cost.

### The measured pass@k → selection@k gap, and what closes it

| Setting | Coverage (pass@k) | Selected (selection@k) | Gap | Selector |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CodeMonkeys, SWE-bench Verified, k=10 | 69.8% | 57.4% (random: 45.8%) | 12.4 pts (~half of random→oracle closed) | test voting → top-3 → selection state machine |
| Barrel of Monkeys ensemble | 80.8% | 66.2% (best member: 62.8%) | 14.6 pts | same |
| LLM Monkeys, MATH, k=10,000 | 98.44% | ~40.5% | ~58 pts | majority voting / RM (no execution) |
| TRAE, SWE-bench Verified | n/a (not disclosed) | 70.6% vs 62.6% best solo | ≥8 pts recovered | regression filter + AST clustering + multi-vote |
| Claude Sonnet 4.5 parallel TTC | n/a | 82.0% vs 77.2% single | 4.8 pts recovered | regression-test rejection + internal scoring model |

What demonstrably closes the gap, in rough order of power: (1) **executing real regression tests** (cheap, high-precision rejection); (2) **model-generated test voting with behavioral clustering** (CodeT, AlphaCode, TRAE); (3) **actively generating *differentiating* tests between surviving candidates** (CodeMonkeys' selection state machine, S*'s adaptive input synthesis) — the only mechanisms that keep improving once candidates agree on easy tests; (4) **learned scoring models trained on execution outcomes** (AlphaCode 2, LEVER, CodeRM, Anthropic's internal scorer). Pure LLM judgment without execution is the weakest and most bias-prone rung.

## Implications for Multiverso design

1. **The selection gap is the product.** CodeMonkeys closed only ~half of the random→oracle gap while spending 5.8% of budget on selection; generation got 78.8%. Multiverso's evidence-aware scheduler should treat the generation/selection split as a *decision variable per Intent*, and the CodeRM result (verification compute pays most on hard instances) says the split must be instance-adaptive. The null hypotheses to beat are exactly fixed best-of-N (CodeMonkeys) and test-only selection (test voting at ~53%).
2. **Evidence hierarchy for admission policies.** Order Evidence by demonstrated reliability: real regression tests > model-generated tests with behavioral clustering > actively generated differentiating tests > learned scoring models > LLM judgment. Judge-only evidence should never be sufficient for ADMIT given documented position/verbosity/self-preference biases — and the trust plane should record generator and judge identities so self-preference (linear in self-recognition) is structurally detectable.
3. **Differentiating-test generation is the highest-leverage novel oracle.** Both winners of the selection problem (CodeMonkeys' selection state machine, S*'s adaptive input synthesis) converge on the same primitive: when candidates disagree, *synthesize an input that separates them and execute it*. Multiverso should implement this as a first-class oracle type whose receipts bind the distinguishing input, both worlds' outputs, and the verdict — this is also the natural bridge to the adversarial-challenge arm of the scheduler.
4. **Heterogeneity is a scheduled action, not a setting.** DEI, TRAE, and Multi-LLM AB-MCTS show cross-family pools raise coverage on SWE tasks; Self-MoA shows mixing hurts when a quality gap exists and the selector is weak. The scheduler should estimate per-family marginal coverage online (AB-MCTS-style) and only pay for heterogeneity when complementarity is observed. PlanSearch adds that diversity should be injected at the *plan* level of the Intent, which maps cleanly onto Multiverso's versioned-Intent → Worlds fan-out.
5. **COMPOSE is open field.** Selection over ensembles-of-systems (Barrel of Monkeys: 66.2% from an 80.8%-coverage pool) works today; *fusing* repo-level patches does not exist yet. AlphaEvolve proves execution-guided recombination works for algorithmic code; LLM-Blender proves generative fusion works for text. A hunk-level COMPOSE guided by per-hunk evidence (which tests exercise which hunks) would be a genuinely novel contribution — and Multiverso's CAS/snapshot data plane is the right substrate for it.
6. **Attested selection is the differentiator, not raw scores.** Anthropic, ByteDance, and Refact.ai already ship best-of-N + selection; none produce auditable evidence of *why* a candidate was admitted, none bind test executions to world state, and none report false-admission rates. Multiverso should benchmark not only resolve-rate but **selection precision** (P(correct | admitted)) — the leaderboards' silence on this metric is the opening.

## Open questions

- **Where does selection saturate?** CodeMonkeys closed ~50% of the random→oracle gap; no published system reports closing much more on SWE-bench. Is the residual gap due to weak oracles (fixable) or genuinely indistinguishable candidates (needs adversarial challenge / human escalation)?
- **Selection under distribution shift:** model-generated tests can be wrong; candidates can overfit to them (reward hacking of the evidence plane). What fraction of CodeMonkeys/TRAE admissions pass model tests but fail hidden tests, and can freshness/adversarial receipts predict it? Not reported anywhere surveyed.
- **Optimal N under fixed budget:** LLM Monkeys gives coverage-vs-N laws; CodeRM gives verification-vs-tests laws; nobody has published the *joint* optimization (N candidates × T tests × S selector turns) that Multiverso's scheduler embodies.
- **Does plan-level diversity transfer from contest code (PlanSearch) to repo-scale edits?** Unmeasured on SWE-bench as of the cutoff.
- **Can behavioral-equivalence clustering (AlphaCode, TRAE AST voting) be made incremental and cache-friendly** so evidence from one world transfers to syntactically distinct but behaviorally identical siblings — a direct fit for Multiverso's CAS?
